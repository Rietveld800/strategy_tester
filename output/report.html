Quickfix strategy · daily · $100k starting capital

Quickfix — portfolio equity curve

Takes profit at a fixed ceiling: a trade is closed at 2.5R, or earlier at the first opposite reversal that sits between entry and 2.5R. The 2.5R ceiling caps every winner. One shared account trading Socrates reversal levels. All 41 markets in the archive were tested, Dec 2025 – Jul 2026; the rules fired on 28 of them, and the rest are listed with zero trades under By market. Capital moves only when a trade closes; each new trade risks 1.175% of liquid capital — the risk that holds this strategy to a 6% maximum drawdown, so every strategy is shown at the same pain. Figures are net of realistic slippage.

How a trade is found
Described for a short; the long side is the exact mirror — bearish levels for the entry, bullish for the target.
1
Rule 1 · Signal
At least 3 bullish reversal levels lie between the previous day's close and today's high — price rose into them and tested them. The lowest of those is the first reversal, the next one up the second.
2
Rule 2 · Clean setup
Refuse the trade if the bar opened at or above the second reversal. Opening below the first, or between the first and the second, is fine; opening above the second means price was already inside the cluster when the day began.
3
Rule 3 · Room to move
The nearest bearish reversal below the entry must be at least 3.5R below it. Any closer and there is not enough room to pay for the risk being taken.
4
Rule 4 · Target
Take profit at 2.5R. If a bearish reversal sits closer than 2.5R below the entry, that level becomes the target instead — price is likely to turn there rather than run through it. The target is recomputed every day, so a newly drawn level can pull it in. 2.5R is a hard ceiling on every winner.
Entry and stop. The bar must close below the first reversal — the proof that price probed the cluster and snapped back. The trade fills at that reversal price, and the stop sits one tick above that bar's high. Position size makes that distance exactly 1.175% of capital, so 1R = 1.175%. One position per market at a time; management starts the day after entry.
Entries come from the reversal levels only. No timing, cycle or aggregate turning-point signal is involved — this is the price half of the Socrates method on its own.

Choosing the profit cap

Final capital by profit cap, levered to a constant 6% drawdown risk solved per cap · does not follow the risk dial

Risk per trade is a free variable, so comparing caps at one risk compares them at unequal pain — part of a wider cap's bigger return is simply a bigger bet. Here the risk is solved for each cap so that every one of them bottoms out at the same 6% maximum drawdown, and the question becomes the one worth asking: for the same hole, which cap ends up with the most money? Every point is a full backtest at its own risk setting.

Quickfixpro — portfolio equity curve

Captures the initial energy move only: the target is fixed at entry, one tick beyond the entry bar's opposite extreme — a tick below its low on a short — and never moves. Stop and target are the two sides of the entry bar, so the bet is simply that its low breaks before its high. One shared account trading Socrates reversal levels. 4
Rule 4 · Target
Take profit one tick below the entry bar's own low. The level is fixed the moment the trade is placed and never moves — no reversal level and no R ceiling is involved. The whole trade is contained by the entry bar: the stop one tick above its high, the target one tick below its low. This is a bet on the initial energy of the move — if the thesis behind the entry is right, that low should break more often than the high does. A winner is worth whatever the entry bar measured, which is usually well under 3.5R.
