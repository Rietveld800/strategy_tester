Backtest on daily bars, net of slippage. Costs are charged as tick slippage, 1 tick on entry, 1 on a limit take-profit or a scheduled close-out, 3 on a stop, converted to R through each trade's own risk distance. The two quickest exits rest hardest on the fill model: Quickfixclose0 assumes one daily bar gives us both a fill at the reversal level intraday and a fill at that day's close, and because the entry trigger requires the close to be beyond the entry price it can only lose when slippage exceeds the move, which is why its drawdown is near zero. Quickfixopen1 assumes the next open is fillable and its stop cannot trade ahead of that open, so an adverse gap costs more than 1R. The three longer holds do carry a live stop on every bar in between, which is what makes them a different question rather than more of the same one, but they inherit the same daily-bar assumption: a stop inside a bar's range is taken as hit before that bar's close. None of this is a free lunch, all of it is the daily-proxy assumption showing through, and intraday prices are what would settle it.
