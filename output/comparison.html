Backtest on daily bars, net of slippage. Costs are charged as tick slippage, 1 tick on entry, 1 on a limit take-profit or a scheduled close-out, 3 on a stop, converted to R through each trade's own risk distance. The shortest hold rests hardest on the fill model: Quickfixopen1 assumes the next open is fillable and its stop cannot trade ahead of that open, so an adverse gap costs more than 1R. Every longer hold does carry a live stop on every bar in between, which is what makes them a different question rather than more of the same one, but they inherit the same daily-bar assumption: a stop inside a bar's range is taken as hit before that bar's close, and a 18-bar sweep out to bar 20 makes that assumption 20 times on a single trade rather than once. One more thing the long holds change on their own: one position per market at a time, so a trade still running on bar 20 blocks every signal that market gives in the meantime, and part of the difference between a short hold and a long one is which setups each was free to take rather than what it made of them. Read the trade counts beside the capital. None of this is a free lunch, all of it is the daily-proxy assumption showing through, and intraday prices are what would settle it.
