Quickfixclose19 equity curve

A fixed 19-bar hold: the position is carried through 19 full price bars and closed at the close of bar 19, with the stop live the whole way. Nothing about the exit reads the market, it is the calendar that closes the trade, so what this measures is how long the move behind the entry keeps paying. All 41 markets in the archive were tested, Dec 2025 – Aug 2026; the rules fired on 28 of them, and the rest are listed with zero trades under By market. Capital moves only when a trade closes; each new trade risks 1% of liquid capital, a chosen figure rather than one solved for a drawdown budget. Figures are net of realistic slippage.

How a trade is found
Described for a short; the long side is the exact mirror, bearish levels for the entry, bullish for the target.
1
Rule 1 · Signal
At least 3 bullish reversal levels lie between the previous day's close and today's high, price rose into them and tested them. The lowest of those is the first reversal, the next one up the second.
2
Rule 2 · Clean setup
Refuse the trade if the bar opened at or above the second reversal. Opening below the first, or between the first and the second, is fine; opening above the second means price was already inside the cluster when the day began.
3
Rule 3 · Room to move
The nearest bearish reversal below the entry must be at least 3.5R below it. Any closer and there is not enough room to pay for the risk being taken.
4
Rule 4 · Target
Close the position at the close of bar 19, 19 bars after the entry bar. The trade is carried through 19 full sessions and is then marked out at the last one's closing price, whatever the price has done in between. The stop is live on every one of those bars and fires ahead of the close on the exit bar, a stop triggers the moment price touches it while a market-on-close waits for the bell. Held this long the exit stops being a reaction to the entry and becomes a fixed holding period: what it asks is whether the edge in the setup is still paying 19 days later, or whether the move was over long before then and the position is only collecting noise.
Entry and stop. The bar must close below the first reversal, the proof that price probed the cluster and snapped back. The trade fills at that reversal price, and the stop sits one tick above that bar's high. Position size makes that distance exactly 1% of capital, so 1R = 1%. One position per market at a time; management starts the day after entry.
Entries come from the reversal levels only. No timing, cycle or aggregate turning-point signal is involved, this is the price half of the Socrates method on its own.
